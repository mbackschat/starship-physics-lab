# ByteByteGo-inspired visual system

Use this as a written style system. It captures the recurring design grammar from the supplied reference set without requiring or redistributing those images.

## Start with one question

Use a large, bold, left-aligned title that names the subject as a question or compact explanation. Place a short colored vertical rule to its left. Skip the introductory paragraph.

## Build a visible reading order

For portrait posters, use four to six numbered sections stacked top to bottom. Give each section a black pill-shaped header that overlaps or sits against a thin rounded border. Put the number inside the pill or in an adjacent black circle.

For landscape diagrams, use fewer and wider stages connected left to right. Keep one dominant flow.

## Give each panel one job

Make each panel explain one mechanism, comparison or consequence. Use thin rules or tinted boxes for internal subdivisions without creating a second hierarchy. The diagram and its labels should communicate the point before the supporting sentence is read.

## Draw nouns and verbs differently

Represent technical nouns as compact, front-facing, flat illustrations with thick dark outlines, simple fills and almost no shading. Keep repeated objects visually identical.

Use arrows, bars and containers for actions and relationships:

- solid arrows for the primary causal path;
- dashed arrows for secondary movement or messages;
- rounded containers for boundaries or ownership;
- bars, tiles and aligned objects for quantities.

Avoid ornamental scenes, dramatic lighting and perspective.

## Keep color semantic

Use a white or faintly tinted ground with black text and borders. Add a restrained family of mint green, sky blue, violet, warm yellow and coral. Assign one accent to one role within a panel. Use pale tints for containers and saturated accents for results, warnings or current state.

Do not rely on color alone. Pair it with a label, icon or position.

## Let typography carry hierarchy

- title: large, bold, left aligned;
- panel pills: uppercase white text;
- subheads: bold black;
- labels: short and adjacent to their objects;
- numbers: visually stronger than their units;
- assumptions and provenance: smallest text on the page, but still readable.

Use a clean sans-serif face. Avoid paragraphs, novelty fonts and rotated copy.

## Prefer comparisons

Place two systems, states or outcomes side by side on a shared baseline. Align like quantities. End a panel with one conclusion badge or bar rather than a list of observations.

## Prompt pattern

Describe the output as a crisp vector-like educational infographic with flat fills, thick dark outlines, rounded panels, black capsule headers, clean sans-serif typography and generous white space. Specify the canvas, reading order, exact copy and the visual mechanism of every panel.

Require no gradients, shadows, photorealism, glossy 3D objects, decorative scenery, logo or watermark. Require original illustrations rather than copied reference art.

## Release checks

- Every required phrase appears once and is spelled correctly.
- All numbers retain their units and signs.
- Equations use the intended symbols and subscripts.
- Bars, proportions and compositions agree with their labels.
- Axes and arrows point in the intended direction.
- No panel is clipped or too dense at the final display size.
- Branding from style references does not appear.
